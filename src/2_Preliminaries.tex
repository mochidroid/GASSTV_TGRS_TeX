\subsection{Notations}
\label{subsec:Notations}
% \vspace{-2mm}
Throughout this paper, we denote vectors and matrices by boldface lowercase letters (e.g., $\NotationVector$) and boldface capital letters (e.g., $\NotationMatrix$), respectively.
We treat an HS image, denoted by $\HSIClean$ with $\NumVert$ vertical pixels, $\NumHori$ horizontal pixels, and $\NumBand$ bands.
We denote the total number of elements in the HS image by $\NumAll = \NumVert \NumHori \NumBand$.
For a matrix data $\NotationMatrix \in \RealSpace{\NumVert \times \NumHori}$, the value at the location $(\IndexPrimal, \IndexDual)$ is denoted by $[\NotationMatrix]_{\IndexPrimal,\IndexDual}$.
The $\ell_{1}$-norm and the $\ell_{2}$-norm of a vector $\NotationVector \in \RealSpace{\NotationNumDim}$ are defined as $\| \NotationVector \|_{1} := \sum_{\NotationIndex=1}^{\NotationNumDim} | \NotationScalar_{\NotationIndex} |$ and $\| \NotationVector \|_{2} := \sqrt{\sum_{\NotationIndex=1}^{\NotationNumDim} \NotationScalar_{\NotationIndex}^{2}}$, respectively, where  $\NotationScalar_{\NotationIndex}$ represents the $\NotationIndex$-th entry of $\NotationVector$.
The nuclear norm of a marix, which is the sum of all the singular values, is denoted by $\| \cdot \|_{*}$.
For an HS image $\HSIClean \in \RealSpace{\NumAll}$, let $\DiffOpVert \in \RealSpace{\NumAll \times \NumAll}$, $\DiffOpHori \in \RealSpace{\NumAll \times \NumAll}$, and $\DiffOpBand \in \RealSpace{\NumAll \times \NumAll}$ be the forward difference operators along the horizontal, vertical, and spectral directions, respectively, with the periodic boundary condition.
Here, a spatial difference operator is denoted by $\DiffOpSp := \begin{pmatrix} \DiffOpVertT & \DiffOpHoriT \end{pmatrix}^{\top} \in \RealSpace{2 \NumAll \times \NumAll}$. 
Using $\DiffOpVert$, $\DiffOpHori$, and $\DiffOpBand$, we denote the second-order spatio-spectral differences by $\DiffOpVert \DiffOpBand \HSIClean \in \RealSpace{\NumAll}$ and $\DiffOpHori \DiffOpBand \HSIClean \in \RealSpace{\NumAll}$.
Other notations will be introduced as needed.



\subsection{Proximal Tools}
\label{subsec:Prox}
In this chapter, we introduce basic proximal tools that play a central role in the optimization part of our method.
Let $\FuncProx$ be a \textit{proper lower semi-continuous convex function}.\footnote{A function $\FuncProx \: : \: \RealSpace{\NumVarProx} \rightarrow ( -\infty, \infty ]$ is called a proper lower semi-continuous convex function if $\lbrace \VarProxOne \in \RealSpace{\NumVarProx} | \FuncProx(\VarProxOne) < \infty \rbrace$ is nonempty, $\lbrace \VarProxOne \in \RealSpace{\NumVarProx} | \FuncProx(\VarProxOne) \leq \alpha \rbrace$ is closed for every $\alpha \in \RealSpace{}$, and $\FuncProx(\lambda \VarProxOne + (1-\lambda)\VarProxTwo) \leq \lambda\FuncProx(\VarProxOne) + (1-\lambda)\FuncProx(\VarProxTwo)$ for every $\VarProxOne, \VarProxTwo \in \RealSpace{\NumVarProx}$ and $\lambda \in (0, 1)$.}
Then, for $\IndexProx > 0$, the \textit{proximity operator} of $\FuncProx$ is defined by
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:Prox}
	\prox_{\IndexProx, \FuncProx}(\VarProxOne) := \arg\min_{\VarProxTwo \in \RealSpace{\NumVarProx}} \FuncProx(\VarProxTwo) + \frac{1}{2 \IndexProx} \| \VarProxOne - \VarProxTwo \|_{2}^{2}.
\end{equation}


The \textit{Fenchel--Rockafellar conjugate function} $\FuncProx^{*}$ of the function $\FuncProx$ is defined by
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:ConjugateFunction}
	\FuncProx^{*}(\VarProxOne) := \sup_{\VarProxTwo} \InnerProduct<\VarProxOne, \VarProxTwo> - \FuncProx(\VarTwo),
\end{equation}
where $\InnerProduct<\cdot , \cdot>$ is the Euclidean inner product.
Thanks to a generalization of Moreau's identity~\cite{Combettes2013Moreau}, the proximity operator of $\FuncProx^{*}$ is calculated as
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:ProxConjugate}
	\prox_{\IndexProx, \FuncProx^{*}}(\VarProxOne) = \VarProxOne - \IndexProx \prox_{\frac{1}{\IndexProx} \FuncProx} \left( \frac{1}{\IndexProx} \VarProxOne \right).
\end{equation}


The indicator function of a set $\SetConvex \subset \RealSpace{\NumVarOne}$, denoted by $\FuncIndicator{\SetConvex}$, is defined as 
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:Indicator_Function}
	\FuncIndicator{\SetConvex} (\VarOne) := 
	\begin{cases}
		0, & \mathrm{if} \: \VarOne \in \SetConvex, \\
		\infty, & \mathrm{otherwise}.
	\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The function $\FuncIndicator{\SetConvex}$ is proper lower semi-continuous convex when $\SetConvex$ is nonempty and closed convex.
The proximity operator of $\FuncIndicator{\SetConvex}$ is equivalent to the projection onto $\SetConvex$, as given by
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:Projection}
	\prox_{\FuncIndicator{\SetConvex}} (\VarProxOne) = \Projection{\SetConvex}(\VarProxOne) := \argmin_{\VarProxTwo \in \SetConvex} \|\VarProxTwo - \VarProxOne \|_{2}.
\end{equation}


\subsection{Primal-Dual Splitting Method (PDS)}
\label{subsec:PDS}
The PDS~\cite{Chambolle2011PDS, Condat2013PDS}, on which our algorithm is based, is an efficient algorithm for solving the following generic form of convex optimization problems:
\begin{equation}
	\label{prob:convex_optim_prob}
	\min_{\VarPrimal, \VarDual} \FuncSmooth (\VarPrimal) + \FuncPrimal (\VarPrimal) + \FuncDual (\VarDual) \text{ s.t. } \VarDual = \LinOpPDS \VarPrimal, 
\end{equation}
where $\FuncPrimal : \RealSpace{\DimVarPrimal} \to (-\infty, +\infty]$ and $\FuncDual : \RealSpace{\DimVarDual} \to (-\infty, +\infty]$ are proper lower semi-continuous convex functions, $\LinOpPDS \in \RealSpace{\DimVarDual \times  \DimVarPrimal}$ is a linear operator, $\FuncSmooth$ is differentiable and its gradient $\nabla \FuncSmooth$ is $\LipsitzConst$-Lipschitz continuous for some real constant $\LipsitzConst > 0$, i.e., $\| \nabla \FuncSmooth(\VarPrimal_{1}) - \nabla \FuncSmooth(\VarPrimal_{2}) \| \leq \LipsitzConst \| \VarPrimal_{1} - \VarPrimal_{2} \|$ for any $\VarPrimal_{1}, \VarPrimal_{2} \in \RealSpace{\DimVarPrimal}$.

These methods solve Prob.~\eqref{prob:convex_optim_prob} by the following iterative procedures:
\begin{equation}
	\label{algo:PDS}
	\AlgoLfloor{
		\begin{array}{l}
			\VarPrimal^{(\IdxAlg+1)} 
			\leftarrow \prox_{\ScalarStepsize{1}, \FuncPrimal}
			\left( \VarPrimal^{(\IdxAlg)} - \ScalarStepsize{1} \bigl( \nabla \FuncSmooth(\VarPrimal^{(\IdxAlg)}) + \LinOpPDS^{\top} \VarDual^{(\IdxAlg)} \bigr) \right), \\
			\VarDual^{(\IdxAlg+1)}
			\leftarrow \prox_{\ScalarStepsize{2}, \FuncDual^{*}}
			\left( \VarDual^{(\IdxAlg)} - \ScalarStepsize{2}
			\LinOpPDS \bigl(2\VarPrimal^{(\IdxAlg+1)} - \VarPrimal^{(\IdxAlg)} \bigr) \right), \\
		\end{array}
	}.
\end{equation}
where $\ScalarStepsize{1}$ and $\ScalarStepsize{2}$ are the stepsize parameters, satisfying
\begin{equation}
	\label{ieq:PDS_ConvergenceCondition}
	\ScalarStepsize{1} \ScalarStepsize{2} \MaxSingularValue{\LinOpPDS^{\top}\LinOpPDS} < 1,
\end{equation}
where $\MaxSingularValue{\cdot}$ is the maximum singular value of $(\cdot)$. Then, the sequences $\left( \VarPrimal^{(\IdxAlg)} \right)_{\IdxAlg \in \mathbb{N}}$ converge to an optimal solution of Prob.~\eqref{prob:convex_optim_prob}~\cite{Chambolle2011PDS, Condat2013PDS}.


\subsection{Weighted Spatial Difference Operator based on Spatial Graph by GSSTV~\cite{Takemoto2022GSSTV}}
\label{subsec:SpatialGraph}
Since we only obtain a noisy HS image in real situations, we construct a spatial graph via a guide image computed by
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:GuideImage}
    \GuideImage(\IndexVert, \IndexHori) :=  \frac{1}{\NumBand} \sum_{\IndexBand=1}^{\NumBand} \HSIObsv(\IndexVert, \IndexHori, \IndexBand),
\end{equation}
where $\HSIObsv$ is a given HS image.
The guide image is averaged in the spectral direction to reduce noise while maintaining the spatial structure. 
Next, we consider to construct a weighted graph $\SpatialGraph(\GuideImage, \SpatialGraphEdgeSet, \SpatialGraphWeightMatTwoD)$ with the edges $\SpatialGraphEdge \in \SpatialGraphEdgeSet$ ($\IndexEdgeOne$ and $\IndexEdgeTwo$ are indices of pixels ($1 \leq \IndexEdgeOne < \IndexEdgeTwo \leq \NumVert \NumHori$)).
The weight matrix $\SpatialGraphWeightMatTwoD \in \RealSpace{\NumSpatialGraphEdge \times \NumSpatialGraphEdge}$ is a diagonal matrix whose entries are the weights $\SpatialGraphWeight \in (0, 1]$ assigned to the edges $\SpatialGraphEdge$, defined as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:SpatialGraphWeight}
    \SpatialGraphWeight := \exp{\left( - \frac{( \GuideImageVec_{\IndexEdgeOne} - \GuideImageVec_{\IndexEdgeTwo})^{2}}{2\ParamSpatialGraph^{2}} \right)},
\end{equation}
where $\ParamSpatialGraph$ is a parameter.
The value of $\SpatialGraphWeight$ is large as the correlation between $\GuideImageVec_{\IndexEdgeOne}$ and $\GuideImageVec_{\IndexEdgeTwo}$ is higher.
We also introduce the incidence matrix $\SpatialGraphIncidenceMatTwoD \in \RealSpace{|\SpatialGraphEdgeSet| \times \NumVert \NumHori}$ for a graph $\SpatialGraph$ as follows: 
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:SpatialGraphIncidenceMat}
	\SpatialGraphIncidenceMatTwoD_{\SpatialGraphEdge, k} = 
	\begin{cases}
		-1 & \text{if } p = k, \\
		1 & \text{if } q = k, \\
		0 & \text{otherwise},
	\end{cases}
	(\forall k = 1, \ldots, \NumVert \NumHori).
\end{equation}
To apply the spatial graph total variation to the entire HS image, we extend the weight matrix $\SpatialGraphWeightMatTwoD$ and the incidence matrix $\SpatialGraphIncidenceMatTwoD$ to block diagonal matrices $\SpatialGraphWeightMat \in \RealSpace{\NumBand |\SpatialGraphEdgeSet| \times \NumBand  |\SpatialGraphEdgeSet|}$ and $\SpatialGraphIncidenceMat \in \RealSpace{\NumBand |\SpatialGraphEdgeSet| \times \NumAll}$, each consisting of $\NumBand $ repetitions of $\SpatialGraphWeightMatTwoD$ and $\SpatialGraphIncidenceMatTwoD$, respectively, defined as:
% To apply the spatial graph total variation to the entire HS image, we extend the weight matrix $\SpatialGraphWeightMatTwoD$ and the incidence matrix $\SpatialGraphIncidenceMatTwoD$. Let $\SpatialGraphWeightMat \in \RealSpace{\NumBand |\SpatialGraphEdgeSet| \times \NumBand  |\SpatialGraphEdgeSet|}$ and $\SpatialGraphIncidenceMat \in \RealSpace{\NumBand |\SpatialGraphEdgeSet| \times \NumAll}$ denote the block diagonal matrices consisting of $\NumBand $ repetitions of $\SpatialGraphWeightMatTwoD$ and $\SpatialGraphIncidenceMatTwoD$, respectively, defined as:
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:ExtentionSpatialGraphMat}
	\SpatialGraphWeightMat := \diag{(\SpatialGraphWeightMatTwoD, \dots, \SpatialGraphWeightMatTwoD)}, \quad
	\SpatialGraphIncidenceMat := \diag{(\SpatialGraphIncidenceMatTwoD, \dots, \SpatialGraphIncidenceMatTwoD)}.
\end{equation}
Then, the weighted spatial graph difference operator for an HS image is given by $\SpatialGraphWeightMat \SpatialGraphIncidenceMat$.
